## Verdict

**REVISE**

The revision closes several concrete round-1 defects, but still cannot implement the frozen registration faithfully. Its cross-file agreement rule substitutes a clock deadline for qualified writer closure. It can silently miss ineligible candidates and retire current-condition faults before observing their recovery. W-self's first-completion and skipped-observation rules remain incomplete. The signal/finalizer protocol also does not establish its claimed whole-job bound or interruption safety.

These need evidence, lifecycle and execution decisions, not a small verbatim editorial patch. This is round 2 of 2: unit 2 stops and goes to the owner under the review cap. This report does not authorize another design round, implementation, repair, deployment, configuration, commissioning or activation.

**New and remaining defects**

1. **High — A completion deadline still becomes writer closure (§§0.4, 1, 3, 7, 8).**

   Registration §3 explicitly rejects an assumed cron finish, repeated hashes, sleep or idle PID as closure. It requires production-capable evidence binding the relevant generation, consumed bytes and interval in which writers could not interleave. §4.2 requires concurrent captures to remain pending/unverifiable until that closure. The registration also expressly says a 06:15 attempt may still be running.

   D2 instead verifies X agreement after 06:15/08:15 and expects the completed state after the deadline to be verified in its capture test. It declares multi-file closure “not claimed” in §7, but those verified results claim agreement without it. Renaming a disagreement “overdue completion” is a useful limited alert; it does not qualify agreement or recovery. Nor does it prove the particular mixed-read mismatch remained incomplete for the whole grace period.

   **Re-run real-writer probe:** retain A's pick (batter 11), privately commit B through the actual scheduler path, then read B's decision (batter 22). The final pick is also 22. The reader consumed an inconsistent pair despite a consistent final state. There is no producer rule preventing that interleaving after the specified deadline. Conversely, old agreeing bytes can clear an incident while a new transaction remains unclosed. This probe exercised the producer/read relation; there is no unit-2 checker yet to execute its verdict.

   D3 uses pick plus state/decision/archives; D5 uses pick plus heartbeat; D6 uses absence across pick/archives/state/decision. They are labelled S, although §0.4 defines S as one file, optionally schedule. Consequently they also bypass the purported cross-file protection. For example, a stale skip suppressor can hide a newer overdue candidate, and a paused producer can expose pick/decision absence inconsistent with a held state sample. The claim that mixed reads can *only* be pending does not follow from these rows.

   **Required decision:** supply separately reviewed producer-capable closure, or retain pending/unverifiable cross-file agreement/recovery with separately qualified overdue alerts and an explicit coverage limitation. W-state's deferral does not discharge this unit's own multi-file obligations.

2. **High — Date closure prevents current-fault recovery, and rebuild forgets older unresolved work (§§0.3–0.4, 1–2, 8).**

   P1/P2 are C-class current conditions, yet §0.3 treats their faults as final and closes the date after its deadline. Closed dates are never evaluated. A missing game or void/current officialDate observation can later be corrected; this design cannot then observe P2/P1 clearing on the original target. Treating the status as “settled” at a deadline repeats the unsupported time-as-evidence assumption. It conflicts with C's recover-when-cleared rule and the registration's fault → repair → recovered → recurrence gate.

   A corrupt/unreadable inventory rebuild opens only the last seven dates. An eight-day-old open date with unavailable evidence or an overdue completion disappears from active inventory, even though normal operation promises no age cutoff. The one-run corruption fault honestly announces damage, but subsequent successful runs have no durable record that older required coverage was lost. Likewise, §0.7 says no eligible games closes the date while §0.3 requires post-deadline closure; it must not retire today's work at the first empty schedule sample or suppress independent dated facts.

   Historical targets need a different, explicit lifecycle. D1 is H but emits verified for a timely receipt of the same selection, contrary to H's “never automatically” rule. **Actual W0 probe:** fault I-late-delivery for 11@71, then VERIFIED with the same incident/date/selection closes that target; W0 has no H barrier. A verified replacement 22@72 correctly does **not** close 11@71. D2 also promises “one per archive” while every archive for the same incident/date/selection maps to the same W0 target (`notify.py:268–295`). Without an event identity or disposition rule, a later refusal can collapse into an already-open event.

   Define current-condition retention/recovery, durable unknown coverage after inventory corruption, and historical event identity/operator disposition separately. Merely leaving H targets open forever does not specify the registered recovery/recurrence gate. These are design gaps, not evidence that the unbuilt predicates have already emitted those notices.

3. **High — The new candidate rule silently misses missing/resume slots and research candidates (§§0.7–0.8, 1–2).**

   P3 waits until the candidate's cutoff and then delegates to D3 or D6. But §0.7 explicitly gives a missing game **no refreshed time**. That candidate's cutoff is therefore undefined; D3 cannot compare it, and D6 requires no pick although this pick exists. The registered candidate-missing-game alert has no path. Waiting for unavailable-input escalation a day later, if reached, is a different incident and does not implement it.

   An undelivered candidate whose game has officialDate != D, but is present and not void, matches neither P1 (committed only) nor P3 (void or missing). It falls to P's verified otherwise despite the registration's explicit candidate resume-day eligibility predicate. The day opportunity-set filter correctly excludes resumed games, but does not implement the selected-candidate fault.

   P3 also delegates to delivery expectations disabled under research. A research candidate that remains void/missing has no independent post-cutoff P fault. Registration W-postponed covers candidates and delivered slots under both intents; the no-delivery exemption cannot clear that independent eligibility check. Both DD legs need these rules, with a qualified bounded observation/age policy for an absent refreshed time.

4. **High — The alarm protocol does not bound its own cancellation/finalizer transition or guarantee temp cleanup (§0.2; W0 root/runner/notifier).**

   The positive part was measured: a real 0.05 s raising SIGALRM interrupted a 0.25 s sleeping sender through actual W0 run_job/flush in about 0.054 s. The notice remained sending, the job lock released, and advancing the fake wall clock past the lease allowed a confirmed fake-ID retry. This supports the ordinary sleep/cancellation/uncertain-send path, not the full proposed finalizer.

   The first alarm is one-shot. Python must unwind nested finally/context-manager cleanup before an outer finalizer can arm its own alarm. That interval has no remaining bound. **Controlled stress probe:** with a 0.03 s real alarm and an injected 0.15 s delay after a real fd close during W0 unwinding, the outer catch was reached at about 0.187 s; the second alarm was not yet armed. This is measured under a deliberately delayed cleanup leaf, not a measured production filesystem stall. It demonstrates that 105 + 10 omits a phase that the design has not bounded. Interpreter/import/root setup before job-lock admission also lacks a bound; “plus interpreter start, inside 120 s” is an assertion, not an enforced limit.

   The atomic-write claim is separately false. `root.py:144` creates the temp before `created = True` at line 145 and before the inner fd-close finally is established. **Side-effect/interruption probe:** call the real os.open for that temp, then raise JobDeadline before returning the fd. Actual write_atomic leaves one owned temp file; the probe closes the orphan fd itself. This models the post-create/pre-assignment interruption window. It does not show an outside-root write or corruption of the destination. It does show that except BaseException alone does not establish the promised cancellation cleanup.

   W0's runner currently returns partial completion/notification information only through a normal JobOutcome; BaseException escapes after unwinding (`runner.py:120–164`). The design must say how the finalizer learns persisted results, completed checks and in-flight work after interruption, how handler/timer state is restored, and how every ordinary error wrapper avoids swallowing JobDeadline. “Ping never raises” must exclude the deadline exception. If the ping succeeds and the final best-effort ping-result write overruns, the stated “finalizer overrun … no ping” is also impossible under the specified order.

   Specify an enforceable whole-invocation/cancellation boundary, interruption-safe owned resource bookkeeping, partial outcome transfer and truthful success/failure-ping semantics. Do not infer that an ordinary sleeping-sender alarm covers all locks, cleanup, C work, response reads and subprocess reaping.

5. **Medium — W-self's observation-completion and first-completion cases remain underspecified (§§0.2, 6).**

   §0.2 marks a *check not started* incomplete. In contrast, a seam call with less than two seconds remaining is skipped and its predicates return unverifiable, without an explicit incomplete outcome. W0 executed_ok means the check produced valid CheckResults without raising; an unverifiable result qualifies (`runner.py:120–135`). Thus invocation success alone cannot distinguish an omitted observation from a performed unavailable observation. In the currently listed fast order, a skipped call in an earlier check would also leave the last self check unstarted under the 60 s rule, making that attempt incomplete; I do not claim a measured all-six-check false success from this scenario. The missing contract is explicit expected date/predicate/input completion accounting, so success does not depend on incidental ordering or a later check exposing an earlier omission.

   E1 leaves no record pending without any bootstrap deadline. There is also no defined E1 case for an existing record whose attempts have all failed/incompletely ended and whose last_completed_at is null. A configured expected job that has never completed must become overdue from a durable installation/admission/due anchor. Otherwise the proposed later multi-job inventory can leave a never-started job pending forever while fast completes and signals success. The named “job missing” and “missing record” tests need those distinct states, not just registry drift.

   Credit: an overdue self fault **can** recover after a completed attempt updates last_completed_at; completed work observing a self fault is still a completion record even though it sends /fail. I do not claim a permanent feedback loop there. The unresolved gap is initial/missing completion and skipped work, plus null/error schema handling.

6. **High — Sampled intent is still presented as continuous historical policy (§0.8, M3/M4).**

   The watchdog knows the config pair only at its polls. A research → enter → research change between two polls is unobserved. A legitimate delivery during enter can then be attributed to research because delivered_at > since + 5 min. Downtime is an even simpler case: the pair is unchanged on return, so since is retained although there were no intervening observations. A long five-minute grace does not prove policy continuity. The scheduler's loaded effective config is another fact that a current file read alone cannot establish for an earlier send.

   This is a reasoning counterexample, not a read or manipulation of Eric's config. Deferring attribution *before* first observation is honest, but does not qualify attribution in later unobserved intervals. Define the observable policy claim and its gap/reset rules; historical attribution needs a bound effective-policy receipt/history or must remain unverifiable. Current config/cron faults can still be checked. M3/M4's C/H label must also choose the lifecycle for each actual result, rather than leaving automatic recovery versus historical permanence to the implementer.

7. **Medium — The churn baseline can remain pending despite a mature healthy history (§5 R2).**

   assess_churn removes every sample older than the longest 180-minute window (`check_heartbeat.py:155–169`). D2 then verifies no-churn only if the oldest *kept* sample is at least 180 minutes old. Both conditions hold only at exact boundary equality, absent a future/invalid timestamp. Normal launch/check timing jitter makes that equality unreliable.

   **Actual-helper probe:** constant-counter samples every five minutes from minute 0 through 180; evaluate at 180 minutes + 1 second. The helper prunes minute 0; the oldest kept sample is 10,501 s old, below the required 10,800 s, so d2 says pending. Repeating near-but-not-exact grid intervals need not ever graduate. This is not evidence of live restart behavior.

   Retain a separate coverage anchor/history with explicit cadence, gap and reset semantics, or define a reviewed boundary tolerance/coverage rule. An old oldest sample also cannot alone prove intervening observation coverage across a long gap. Keep actual positive churn thresholds active during initialization; baseline uncertainty concerns no-churn certification, not suppression of evidenced churn.

8. **Medium — The new validators omit normal production shapes and leave “valid” evidence underspecified (§0.5, §§1–5).**

   The source has **five** heartbeat states including stalled (`heartbeat.py:24–30`), not four. A real write_heartbeat(STALLED) probe produces that state, and the monitor treats it as stale. R1 can still report that fault through its separate read, but the shared validator would reject a legitimate production liveness artifact for D5/R7.

   R1's direct helper also does not implement §0.5's structural-invalid → unavailable rule. **New helper probes:** JSON `[]` raises AttributeError at its `.get`; a running object with a naive timestamp raises TypeError when subtracting the aware clock (`check_heartbeat.py:49–59`). Literal reuse therefore produces a generic checker failure for those inputs rather than a qualified unavailable R1 result. Specify the direct-read adapter/error disposition without treating a separate validated read as proof about the helper's later bytes.

   DailyPick.slot_results is dict **or None**, default None (`picks.py:225–226`). The real private-commit probe retains slot_results = null. The table only defines its keys/values; it must explicitly permit null/absent compatible ungraded state rather than requiring keys from every healthy ungraded pick.

   finalized_at is merely “parseable,” while M4 compares it against an aware since. Naive or wrong-type timestamps need a specified unavailable disposition. Archive validation says “its time key” without naming the three envelope schemas/time fields, types or selection/cutoff evidence. “Valid skip,” “valid commit,” and a committed primary/double_down must have exact action/source/status/leg semantics; a structural allowance of primary = None is not yet an applicable committed selection. F8's completion deadline is unspecified between the scheduler/result deadlines. Missing optional records on a legitimate no-games early return also need a disposition compatible with unavailable precedence (`scheduler.py:2579–2590` returns before new day-state/finalization work).

   **Re-run compatibility probes:** the malformed numeric-date decision still parses, and a pick with bluesky_posted = 'false' and no URI is still truthy to pick_was_delivered. D2's extra date/type/receipt requirements address those original cases. The remaining issues concern the new layer's exact production-domain and applicability rules, not changing those compatibility parsers.

9. **Medium — Observation loss resets unresolved timers, and delivery cadence/predicate coverage is narrowed (§0.3, D3/D4/D6, W-deliver registration).**

   §0.3 removes seen conditions when they “stop being observed.” An unavailable read does not prove clearance. Observe an unresolved attempt for 14 minutes, lose its pick/schedule input for one poll, then observe the same unresolved attempt again: the literal removal rule restarts its 15-minute timer. Repeated read failures can postpone that alert indefinitely, although no repair was observed. Inventory corruption resetting timers is explicitly disclosed; ordinary unknown observations must not silently have the same effect. Preserve the anchor until qualified clearance or explicit disposition.

   Registration W-deliver separately alerts a scoreable locked_unconfirmed commit without a receipt. D4 covers only delivery_attempted true and waits 15 minutes; no row uses that decision status. If the marker is false/absent or the current pick is unavailable, the named delivery predicate is lost. Decision absence/mismatch is a separate incident. Define its evidence and pending/overdue policy explicitly.

   D3 and D6 require two **post-cutoff** polls. For a fault that begins at an off-grid cutoff, the first successful poll remains non-alerting and the second can be nearly ten minutes later, plus check duration. Registration §3 promises the next successful poll, up to five minutes plus duration, for a persistent crossed deadline. Persistence must be bound to actual pre-existing overdue/producer evidence or explicitly re-dispositioned; two poll confirmations must not be advertised as the existing five-minute bound. These are reasoning counterexamples to the proposed rules, not executed unit-2 timings.

10. **Medium — Cron token presence still counts commands that do not run (§4, §5 R3).**

    A standard five-field line with command `echo /path/uv run bts check-pick-entered` contains every prescribed token but runs echo. So does non-executing text after an inline shell comment, for example `echo ok # /path/uv run bts check-pick-entered`. Under enter this can falsely establish a checker; under research it can falsely report an active-checker breach. Skipping only full comment lines does not address it. The named non-executing-comment test conflicts with the stated lexer.

    Define an executable installed form, shell/comment/wrapper handling and the relevant unsupported scope. Unrelated legitimate cron forms such as @reboot must not automatically become evidence of an unsupported entry checker just because they lack five time fields. The supported heartbeat command also needs execution qualification, not mention-only qualification. No installed crontab was read here.

11. **High — The expanded test plan still cannot establish gates 1–3 or resolve the outstanding design choices (§8).**

    The named cases are a substantial improvement, but capture's green assertion must use qualified production-capable closure, not “after the deadline.” Add a producer paused across 06:15/08:15, old agreeing inputs against an unclosed new transaction, and each multi-file row currently labelled S. Explicitly assert no false recovered notice.

    Also pin cases for a repaired P1/P2 after its date would close; corrupt inventory with work older than seven days; a live missing game with no refreshed cutoff; a resume-day candidate and a research-mode invalid candidate; every missed seam observation despite executed_ok; no record and an existing null last-completion record past bootstrap; unknown-input timer retention; mode downtime/inter-poll transitions; churn jitter and long gaps; normal null slots/stalled heartbeat; echo/inline-comment cron; temp creation before bookkeeping; delayed deadline unwinding; and ping success followed by final record failure. Historical refusal/late-event identity, disposition and recurrence need explicit expected notices.

    The owner must decide the evidence and lifecycle contracts before those tests can be written with defensible green assertions. A mutant count and the 150 passing W0 tests do not supply those decisions.

**Explicit deferrals and buildability**

Registration §2 allows “deferred/unverifiable with reason” in the 119-record coverage matrix; it does not require pretending every trigger is implemented. The scheduled-check half, loaded-code version and actual poll-progress deferrals are honest and acceptable **as declared limited coverage** for this design review. R7's liveness replacement is accurately scoped. The pre-observation historical-mode limitation is also honest, but later unobserved intervals remain unqualified as finding 6 explains. These dispositions must appear in the eventual complete W9 matrix and operational owner record; this unit has not produced or approved that matrix, nor any A8 residual decision.

The multi-file disclaimer is different: it conflicts with X verified/recovery and the S classifications while retaining those implementations. It cannot waive registration §3/§4.2 through wording alone. Likewise a C-fault closure or seven-day rebuild is not an accepted recovery/coverage policy merely because it is described.

Buildability remains blocked by choices about closure evidence, unknown versus cleared observations, C/H event lifetime, lost inventory coverage, missing-game aging, first-completion aging, historical policy attribution and interruption-safe execution. They affect facts asserted and notifications delivered, so they cannot be resolved as routine implementation details. Transport seam mechanics and most compatibility validation are now concrete enough to build once those contracts are resolved. §0.6's “no re-certification is triggered” is premature: registration's final-diff/call-graph review decides that; unchanged production dm.py alone does not.
