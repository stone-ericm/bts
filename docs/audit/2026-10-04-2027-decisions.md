## D1. What should the system optimize next season?
**The choice.**
- **Option 1:** keep chasing the best chance of 57, using the season best only to break ties.
- **Option 2:** accept a stated reduction in the projected jackpot chance in exchange for a better longest streak.

The plan requires both outcomes side by side in a table, with no unexplained mixture weight.

**New fact for this choice (W3, verified from the 2026 Official Rules §§7–8):** the $10,000 Top Streak Prize goes to the highest eligible streak of 20 or more, ties split, and it is paid only if no Grand Prize is awarded. Three different things could be optimized: the chance of winning that prize, the expected share of it, or our own expected season best. They can rank policies differently. We have no model of the field's best streak or of ties, so the prize objectives cannot yet be computed (W3 §1). 2026's best was 18, below the prize floor.

**Evidence.**

*What the field did in 2026* (W2.1, a valid census of all 121,852 entrants):
- The best season streak was 39 (one entrant).
- 2,186 entrants reached 20 or more; 49 reached 30 or more; none reached 40.
- Our 18 sat at the 96.2–97.5 percentile.
- One entrant's 39 was above the Top Streak prize's floor of 20. Whether that entrant was eligible and was paid is not established by our capture (eligibility, the official closing standings and the no-Grand-Prize condition all apply).

*What the policies do*, on the trusted evaluator: realized replay of serving-realistic profiles, 2021–2025, 120 season trajectories, no probability haircut (`docs/audit/2026-07-13-dd-p-policy-value-sensitivity.md`, register X-04):

| Policy | Chance of reaching 20 in a season | Chance of 57 |
|---|---|---|
| Deployed reach-57 policy | 31.7% | not measurable (no trajectory reached 57; about 0.01% on the analytic estimate) |
| Always double | 40.8% | not measurable |
| No doubling at low streaks | 20.0% | not measurable |
| Always single | 7.5% | not measurable |

These are historical replays on profiles, not 2026 outcomes. The doubling policies' advantage is larger on realized sequences than any iid model suggests, because real hit sequences have run structure.

*The tail period* (9/03 onward, P-05) validated that the E[season-best] objective was applied correctly; it says nothing about its value.

**Reading it (reasoning, not a measurement):**
- The jackpot side of this trade is too small to measure for every policy, so Option 1 protects a quantity that cannot be distinguished from zero.
- The season-best side moves visibly with how often we double.
- A prize objective needs both reaching 20 and beating the field's best; 2026's best was 39.
- This is Eric's call: the chance of 57, the chance of reaching 20, the expected season best, or the prize.

**Cost if wrong:** trading away jackpot chances that were real, or keeping an objective whose projections are uncertain.

## D2. When is evidence strong enough to change how we pick?
**The situation.** The old README headline (86% best-pick accuracy) came from a backtest that knew how many times each batter actually came to the plate. Live, the 2026 recommendations went 92/125 (73.6%) on primary picks and 69/102 on double-down legs, contest-graded.
- **W1.2:** on the 2026 served-slate window, the same actual-PA method scored 84% on its diagnostic top picks; with expected instead of realized plate-appearance counts it scored 65%, and the other diagnostic surfaces, including what was served, 67–71%. These are diagnostics on a restricted window. They do not reproduce the historical headline, and they assign none of its gap to any one cause.
- **W1.3:** within the window, the realized-count oracle is the largest contrast. Stated minus realized was +0.031 across known candidates and +0.060 at the top pick, and the recalibration intercept departs from identity. These describe these populations; they are not a uniform offset. Day-to-day ranking skill is modest (within-slate AUC 0.566). Whether it changed over the window is undetermined. Drift, ball regime and luck were not identified as causes, and the diagnostics cannot rank the explanations.

**The choice.**
- **Option 1:** keep the current system until a fresh season confirms something better.
- **Option 2:** allow one *named* change backed by held-out forecast evidence and stress tests, accepting that its effect on the chance of 57 cannot be verified from this sample.

**Recommendation (reasoning, not a measurement): Option 2, narrowly.**
- Only a change that passes a held-out 2027 test before it touches decisions.
- The clearest candidate is a calibration correction for the too-high probabilities (W4 rank 4a). It has to clear a held-out proper-score gate first. Because the skip and double rules use probability thresholds, it also needs a separate policy replay before it may change what gets played.
- Under D3, nothing measured on 2026 counts as validation.

**Cost if wrong:** a change that looks better on forecasts but plays worse, or a known bias left in place for another season.

- **W2.3 (MLB's own forecast):** the comparison used MLB's "% chance to hit" from its most-selected-players sheet, which covered about 9 of our roughly 250 daily candidates. MLB's all-player sheet carries the same number for every player, but with no round id, so which game it refers to is unresolved and it was not read. On the covered candidates (63 dates, 7/04–9/27), MLB's number scored slightly better than ours on proper scores, and both stated more than came true, ours by about 3 pp more. Neither ranked a day's candidates well, and the top-pick difference spans zero. In-sample, MLB's number carried information beyond ours. Nobody has established what MLB's number predicts (starting, a plate appearance or an at-bat), so this is association only.
- **W2.2 (an early-season field cohort):** against 310 top players from the May 1 board, on 45 shared dates (May–July), our hit rate was 73.2% and theirs 66.4%; the difference, +6.9 pp [−6.4, +19.6], includes zero. It is conditional on an unverified name-based attribution of their pick files and is not a skill ranking.
- **#87 (leaderboard mechanisms):** too little data to test any mechanism: no cell reached the testability floor, and none was nominated. On the same slots, the public consensus pick did +4.2 pp [−5.4, +12.8] against ours. That is descriptive only; public pick logs don't prove the consensus was visible before our lock, so it says nothing about the value of copying.
