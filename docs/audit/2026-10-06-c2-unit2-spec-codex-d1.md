## Verdict

**REVISE**

The design cannot yet faithfully implement the frozen registration. In particular, the two-minute bound is not enforced, W-self can signal successful completion of skipped work, a failed final skip write can remain green indefinitely, and cross-file verdicts can report healthy commits as inconsistent. W-restart's deployment and polling predicates do not establish their stated facts. These require decisions about evidence and execution contracts, rather than a few verbatim editorial fixes.

This is a design verdict. No unit-2 implementation, deployment, installed monitor, live commissioning or operational acceptance was tested or approved.

## Findings

1. **High — The 120 s whole-job budget is not an execution bound (§0.1, §0.4–0.5, §6, §8).**

   Giving calls and the notifier a remaining-time allowance does not interrupt an admitted call. W0 explicitly leaves this unresolved: `src/bts/watchdog/notify.py:37–38`; `flush()` checks elapsed time only before calling `self.send(...)`, at lines 388–395. Its state-lock acquisition is blocking (`notify.py:246–247`, `root.py:194–210`), and its flush clock starts after the first claim transaction (`notify.py:365–376`). Reading/validating an unbounded notification history, listing/reading archives, and result/self persistence also have no specified deadline mechanism.

   **Synthetic execution:** a notifier with a 1 s budget admitted a fake sender that advanced the injected monotonic clock by 130 s, then recorded its message ID as sent. This tested the actual W0 flush path without waiting or using a transport. Separately, the actual `bts.dm.send_dm`/`retry_urlopen` chain, with credentials and HTTP patched at their leaves, consumed **195 simulated seconds** in an allowed sequence: third-attempt success for authentication, handle resolution and conversation lookup, followed by one successful message send. Each of the first three stages can consume three 15 s calls plus 5 s and 10 s retry sleeps (`dm.py:49,65,72,87`; `util.py:34–56`). This is simulated elapsed time, not a network measurement.

   The formula `max(0, remaining − 5 s)` can also increase the inherited 90 s notifier budget to approximately 115 s when little predicate time was used. It must be capped by both the notifier limit and the actual remaining job allowance. The final 5 s is not assigned a bounded ping/persistence contract; the ping could itself overrun the deadline. `urllib` timeout plus unrestricted response reads is not a declared whole-response deadline.

   **Effect:** a hung send or state lock can exceed two minutes, retain the job lock, and starve later checks. Starting another seam with a nominal timeout does not close this boundary. Registration §3's execution bound and §4.3's hung-work gate remain unimplemented in the design.

2. **High — W-self's success rule does not attest completion of all due work, and its record cannot support its own queries (§6, §0.1, §8).**

   The success-ping conditions omit `budget_exhausted == false`, completion of every due check/date, and overdue/lock-starved due jobs. The exhausted-budget rule manufactures `unverifiable` results for predicates that were not performed. Those results can persist, the notifier can run without an infrastructure error, and there can be no checker failure or old pending notice: every stated success-ping condition then holds. Healthchecks would receive success for unfinished work.

   W-self's own overdue/lock-starvation results are business `fault` results under W0. A successfully delivered W-self fault leaves no pending notice; it does not fail the listed ping conditions. When later jobs are added, `fast` can therefore keep the dedicated check healthy while a due job is disabled. Looking only at *registered* jobs also cannot identify a job/check removed from the observed registry. The expected due-work inventory must have a separate definition. Ordinary detected business faults, such as a late pick, must still allow a completion ping when their checks and notification work actually completed.

   `self/<job>.json` is described as one overwritten last-attempt record. Its fields contain neither `last_completed_at` nor a consecutive-lock-skip count/history, yet the self check requires both. Overwriting a completed attempt with one skip loses the last completion; overwriting each skip loses the information needed to count three. A skip writer racing the running attempt's completion also needs an attempt identity and ordering rule. Missing/corrupt records, first installation, failed self-record persistence, and the ordering of final record versus ping are unspecified.

   The **commissioning boundary is correct**: Eric's dedicated check and a missed-completion rehearsal are operational requirements, not prerequisites to a synthetic code signature. Missing URL or failed ping being recorded without changing the exit code is compatible with that boundary. However, a local self check cannot observe its own death; the external check is meaningful only if its successful signals are qualified completion signals. The stated period/grace must also be consistent between the local overdue rule and the commissioned check, or their different detection bounds must be explicit.

3. **High — Persistent finalization gaps survive the specified W-decision rules (§1, §3, inherited draft §1/§3).**

   **Failed skip publication:** W-deliver reports `verified` when there is no decision, `final_skip_candidate` is set and `committed_pick_written` is false. W-decision's next-day unresolved rule excludes days with skip evidence. Neither rule expires the pending skip at the declared 06:15 deadline. `_write_endofday_skip` uses the best-effort `write_decision`, which returns `None` on publication failure; the scheduler retains the pending skip (`scheduler.py:759–783`, `daily_decision.py:61–106`). **Synthetic execution:** failing that writer's atomic-write leaf left no decision and a persisted MDP skip candidate with `committed_pick_written=false`. Applying the specified predicates to this production-shaped state yields no finalization fault, even after 06:15. A pending policy choice is not the registration's applicable recorded final skip.

   **Attempt grace has no initial anchor:** the first durable `delivery_attempted=true` save occurs before the send; `delivered_at` and `pick_locked_at` are still null (`scheduler.py:1056–1068`, and the public branch at 1084–1095). A crash before the response can preserve exactly this state. **Synthetic execution of the actual delivery path** observed that tuple inside a fake sender. The inherited `delivered_at + 5 min` / `pick_locked_at + 5 min` rule cannot time it. A restart later stamps `pick_locked_at`, so that timestamp is not an immutable attempt-start time either. Define how a missing matching decision is aged without restarting its grace on every observation/restart; unbound attempt evidence must remain unavailable, with a bounded missing-completion alert.

   **Private finalization:** `pick_locked=true` alone is also set for non-delivered classification locks (`scheduler.py:2735–2761`). It is not independent proof of a private commit. The actual private path saves the pick and lock before publishing its decision (`scheduler.py:1019–1030`). A missing private decision therefore needs an explicit pending/unverifiable and overdue-completion disposition, not an inferred healthy private lock.

   Finally, the inherited score-bearing-preview predicate uses any non-null `result`. The production type includes `unresolved` and `suspended`, alongside terminal results (`picks.py:225`). Those labels alone do not establish streak-bearing scoring. State exactly which terminal/slot evidence is a scoring fault and distinguish incomplete result observations.

4. **High — Deferring W-state does not authorize mixed-file W1 verdicts (§0.3, §1–3, §8).**

   The spec says it reads each file once, at different instants, and assigns consistency to deferred W-state. But W-decision selection comparisons, private locking, skip suppression and delivery/decision agreement are already multi-file verdicts. Registration §2 requires transient in-progress commits to be pending; §4.2 expressly requires concurrent commit/scoring fixtures to remain pending/unverifiable until qualified closure. This obligation is independent of implementing the W-state replay.

   **Synthetic execution using real writers:** the reader retained candidate A's bytes; the scheduler then saved and privately committed B through `_deliver_and_lock_pick`; the reader obtained B's decision. The held pick had batter 11, the later decision batter 22, and the final persisted pick batter 22. The completed production-shaped state agreed, but the specified comparison of the two consumed files would report `I-selection-mismatch`. Atomic replacement protects each file individually, not the relation between them (`picks.py:303–320`, `scheduler.py:668–723,1019–1030`, `util.py:11–31`). Conversely, a mixed read can pair old agreeing evidence and falsely clear an incident while a new transaction is unfinished.

   Name the production-capable closure evidence for each cross-file conclusion, or constrain that conclusion to pending/unverifiable and separately alert overdue completion. A five-minute sleep/grace, repeated equal bytes, or an assumed idle scheduler does not prove closure. Pause/kill actual producers between their separate saves and test the real CLI's notification outcome, as the registration requires. Unreadable non-atomic archives during `archive.write_text` must not be interpreted as absent or as completed abandonment (`scheduler.py:2131–2146`).

5. **High — Prior-date discovery and incident recovery depend on the notifier rather than complete work inventory (§0.2, §1–6).**

   Today/yesterday plus open notification targets misses unresolved dates that never opened a fault: W0 does not create targets for `pending` or `unverifiable` (`notify.py:268–295`). If the checker misses a date's closing opportunity and is disabled for several days, that date has no notification target to carry when checking resumes. Even an open incident is no longer evaluated once it ages beyond three days. Registration §3 says to carry unfinished prior-date incidents across midnight; it does not authorize this silent three-day cutoff. Reading today's/yesterday's data after notification-state failure does not restore the omitted inventory.

   Carrying only a **date** is also insufficient for W0's selection-bound recovery. Recovery requires the same check/date/selection, and usually the same incident (`notify.py:277–289`). **Synthetic execution:** a verified replacement `22@72` did not recover the open fault for `11@71`. This is correct W0 behavior, but unit 2 needs rules for retired candidates, replacement selections, day-level versus slot-level results, and historical refusal/late-send incidents. Re-reading only the current pick cannot emit those qualified recoveries. Restart/self rows always labelled today also need a rule to close previous-date episodes after recovery.

   Declare an independent unresolved-date/selection inventory and an explicit aging/disposition policy. Preserve unknown past work when inputs or notification state are unavailable. Separate historical incidents that require operator disposition from current conditions that can genuinely recover; do not manufacture an all-clear merely because the current candidate changed.

6. **High — W-deliver narrows the opportunity and receipt rules, and widens the warning window (§0.5–0.7, §1, §7.2; inherited draft §1).**

   **Last scheduled game is not last eligible opportunity.** `is_regular_season_game` filters only `gameType` (`util.py:59–66`); postponed/cancelled games and resumed games remain regular-season rows. The inherited no-disposition predicate closes at the schedule's last game. On an all-rainout/resume-only slate it can fault despite there being no eligible opportunity. On a mixed slate a late cancelled/resumed game can postpone the alarm beyond the last genuinely eligible cutoff. Explicitly qualify eligibility before constructing the day opportunity set. Malformed/unavailable eligibility must not become an empty no-games control.

   **Confirmed timing is unnecessarily decision-gated.** The only late-receipt row requires both `decision.delivery_status == delivered` and `pick_was_delivered(pick)`. A qualified delivered pick with a late `delivered_at` and missing/invalid/mislabelled decision does not reach this row. W-decision's missing-decision alert is a different incident and does not satisfy W-deliver's registered late-confirmation predicate. Observe independently qualified receipt timing even when the decision comparison is unavailable; compare the earliest actual selected DD leg.

   **Pre-cutoff coverage is incomplete and overbroad in time.** The frozen row covers a scheduled check/wake that cannot precede cutoff. §7.2 expressly implements only the current sleeping wake and drops the scheduled-check observation because planned times are not persisted. That is an honest evidence limitation, but it is still a narrowed registration predicate requiring a prerequisite or an explicit accepted unavailable/deferred disposition. The inherited wake rule also lacks the registration's restriction that warnings begin in the existing entry window. The entry CLI defaults to 75 minutes before first pitch (`cli.py:1641–1642`); the spec would warn much earlier whenever a candidate and a late wake coexist. Pin the applicable window and distinguish a fresh current wake from a stale/unbound heartbeat.

   **Prior-date freshness also changes the observation bound.** §0.5 permits a 30-minute schedule cache for yesterday/carried dates. A late game or status/time transition across midnight can therefore remain unobserved through several otherwise successful five-minute polls. Declare the cache's actual observation time and this added delay; either refresh evidence needed by active prior-date incidents at the registered cadence or obtain an explicit disposition for the narrowed detection bound. Cached current-status evidence must not be labelled as a fresh observation at this run's time.

   The regular-season filter correctly prevents expecting picks merely because the current postseason has games. Under valid research intent, not expecting recommendation delivery is also correct. These exemptions must not clear independent corruption, current-slot eligibility, mode or liveness checks.

7. **High — W-restart's deploy and post-restart rows infer facts their evidence cannot establish (§5, §0.4).**

   `git log -1 --format=%H%x20%ct` supplies a commit's commit time, not when that tree was installed and not which revision the running process loaded. A commit created last week can be installed today without a restart and have a timestamp older than the still-running unit. A commit created only five minutes after the unit started also never trips the `>10 minutes after ActiveEnterTimestamp` comparison, even hours after a failed restart. Conversely, future-dated commits can trip it after a successful restart. These are reasoning counterexamples; no live deployment was observed. Calling this the strongest available deployment observation does not make `I-stale-deploy` a proven fault. Bind an actual install/change observation and loaded process revision, or register a limited/unverifiable deploy disposition.

   A heartbeat later than unit start proves at most that a heartbeat was written. `run_day` writes one **before** fetching the schedule (`scheduler.py:2572–2578`); result polling is a later conditional path (`scheduler.py:3108–3137`). The `I-no-poll-after-restart` row checks for **no heartbeat**, not absent required polling with a fresh heartbeat. It neither defines when polling is due nor binds observed progress to the current unit/process/selection. Register an explicit qualified progress test or a deferred/unverifiable polling disposition. No-games sleeping is a valid separate case where polling should not be demanded.

   The multiple-instance parent/child rule addresses the documented `uv`/Python double-counting problem. However, the spec's claimed box observations and installed unit details were not independently verified in this review; they are authored fixture assumptions. The `ps` parser must bind actual entry-point invocations, not arbitrary argv substrings, and unavailable/malformed `unit`/`head` output must have their own unverifiable results rather than fall through to `verified`. An active unit and a fresh heartbeat cannot substitute for either progress or loaded-version evidence.

8. **Medium — The external heartbeat/churn dependency is only partly qualified (§5, §0.3–0.4).**

   A marker-bearing cron line mentioning `scripts/check_heartbeat.py` does not verify five-minute cadence, the target heartbeat/unit/state path, enabled churn or an effective independent ping. Examples include `--no-churn`, a different `--churn-unit`, a custom `--churn-state`, or a line without the ping option. The installed script permits these configurations (`check_heartbeat.py:217–228`). The frozen W-restart row requires unavailable counters/**monitor configuration** to be surfaced, not just command presence.

   A fresh monitor state-file mtime proves that the file was replaced, not that a counter was observed. The actual monitor calls `save_churn_samples` even when `read_nrestarts` returns `None` (`check_heartbeat.py:237–247`). **Synthetic execution** produced a freshly written `{unit: bts-scheduler, samples: []}` with reason `nrestarts unavailable (churn check skipped)`. This can keep the freshness proxy green while the credited external churn observation is unavailable. Validate the dependency's configuration and actual sample coverage, with explicit limits on what its local state can attest about its external signal.

   The reused `assess_churn` deliberately discards malformed samples and treats no usable history as a zero delta. **Synthetic execution:** malformed history plus counter 999 returned `False, 'nrestarts 999 (+0 in window)'`. A fresh counter is not evidence of a clean retrospective window when its baseline is missing. Define initialization, reset, corrupt history and insufficient-window coverage as such; do not report historical verified agreement from that helper return alone.

   Reusing the pure threshold logic and not calling the shared-state-writing `main()` are correct. But `is_stale(path, now)` itself calls `path.read_text()` (`check_heartbeat.py:45–59`). Together with the common heartbeat snapshot and the restart/wake comparisons, this conflicts with the stated once-per-file snapshot unless an explicit held-byte reader/adapter is defined. Validate its object/timestamp shapes too: valid JSON is not sufficient input qualification.

9. **Medium — W-mode requires an unpersisted intent epoch and ignores real untagged entry cron (§4; inherited draft §4).**

   The inherited delivery-in-research rule depends on the date being at/after intent was set. `check_entry_intent` returns only intent, effective delivery and problems (`entry_intent.py:20–55`); none of the specified inputs persists when a policy took effect. A legitimate delivery yesterday, followed by switching to research today, can become a breach under the unconditional decision-status branch. Date-level comparisons also cannot separate a same-day legitimate pre-transition delivery from a post-transition breach. The reverse transition can similarly make yesterday's private commit appear to be a private-under-enter fault. Config file mtime is not a qualified history of intent. Supply a reviewed effective-policy epoch/receipt, or mark historical mode attribution unverifiable while checking current config and independently bound current behavior.

   The cron rule counts only `# BTS-HETZNER` lines. An uncommented entry checker without that management marker still runs and is a research-mode breach, but is declared absent by the spec. Conversely, the command string inside a comment/echo or otherwise non-executing shell context must not count as an active checker. Define the supported installed-cron parsing/scope and treat unsupported active command forms as unavailable, rather than claiming no checker. Registration R4/W-mode refers to the actual installed entry checker, not only installer-tagged text.

   Using the actual `check_entry_intent`/scheduler resolver is appropriate: explicit-key precedence, aliases, private fallback and the legacy `shadow_mode` refusal are implemented (`scheduler.py:882–916`). Preserve independent checks when config is unreadable/invalid; the configuration fault is not a reason to skip liveness or completion observation.

10. **High — Production parsers are not the required qualification layer (§0.3, §0.5–0.6, §1–4).**

    Rejecting a present decision only when `parse_decision` returns `None` is insufficient. That parser checks a recognized schema tag, action, boolean scoreable and merely the presence of `date` (`daily_decision.py:132–147`). **Synthetic execution** accepted `{schema_version: bts_daily_decision_v3, date: 123, action: skip, source: mdp, scoreable: false}` with no finalization time or delivery status. The inherited MDP-skip row can then report verified from semantically invalid and unbound evidence. Validate expected ET date, field types, status meaning, source, finalization time and the applicable selection shape after parsing.

    `parse_daily_pick` similarly constructs dataclasses without runtime field validation. **Synthetic execution** round-tripped a pick with `bluesky_posted='false'` and no URI; `pick_was_delivered` returned true (`picks.py:323–331,483–513`). Dataclass construction is not receipt validation. Distinguish a production compatibility flag from a qualified confirmed receipt; malformed delivery fields, identities/times or cross-date records are unavailable evidence.

    Specify object/field validation for scheduler state, archive envelopes and schedule responses as well. Successful `json.loads`, missing keys treated as empty lists, or a missing `officialDate` must not silently establish no games/eligibility/skip agreement. For selection comparisons, the single-record runner-up caveat is valid, but it does not excuse a pick file containing an actual DD leg when the committed decision says `single`, or a missing real DD leg under `double`. Test action/leg count and roles in addition to batter/game identity. An absent pick needed to verify a scoreable decision is unavailable, not an all-clear.

11. **Medium — The five-command seam is a useful allowlist, but does not contain the inherited transport's process path (§0.4, §6, §8).**

    W0's CLI calls `bts.dm.send_dm` (`watchdog/cli.py:54–57`). That calls the posting password resolver; without the supported environment credential it tries two `security find-generic-password` subprocesses, without timeouts (`dm.py:15–22,36`; `posting.py:60–103`). These processes are outside the five names. The spec neither forbids this fallback for the watchdog nor assigns it to a seam. Patching the entire DM transport in gates conceals it. This is a static call-graph finding; no password getter or keychain process was invoked in the review.

    The process table covers the proposed crontab, counter, unit and process-tree observations. Its `head` command cannot supply loaded-version evidence (finding 7). The table is not intrinsically minimal: the two `systemctl show` observations could share one call, although retaining the exact `read_nrestarts` adapter is a reasonable compatibility choice. Do not add process commands simply to give the flawed timestamp predicate an appearance of coverage. Declare an environment-only bounded watchdog credential/transport path, or another explicitly reviewed boundary, with missing credentials remaining pending and retryable.

    MLB, DM authentication/chat and the dedicated Healthchecks ping are three distinct allowed network leaves. Only MLB has a new detailed seam contract here. Specify the DM and ping deadline/response/failure contracts as well; no predicate may call production status helpers that perform an additional MLB fetch behind the shared schedule seam. State the remaining direct-child boundary honestly: substituting `proc.run` in the kernel gate tests the watchdog caller, not the real binaries. Direct seam tests must cover bounded stdout **and stderr**, timeout termination/reaping, exact argv and environment, nonzero/error shapes and the rejected process paths.

12. **High — The proposed test plan does not close registration gates 1–3 (§8 and the inherited cases).**

    The real CLI, leaf substitutions, real producer writers, red controls and per-predicate recovery/recurrence shape are good foundations. The existing W0 suite passing does not test the new predicates or the new completion protocol. Add named red/green fixtures or mutants for the defects above:

    - **Completion/deadline:** a budget-exhausted run with persisted placeholder results must not send success; a disabled/removed due check/job, blocked state lock and hung admitted sender must be detected while generic cron and scheduler pings stay healthy. A correctly completed business fault with a confirmed fake DM ID must still produce a completion signal. Exercise concurrent self-record writers, three skips, missing/corrupt records and failed record persistence.
    - **Finalization:** kill/fail the real skip writer after pending skip state is saved, then cross 06:15; pause the real send after its durable attempt marker; restart without refreshing the grace indefinitely; pause a private commit before decision publication. Include nonterminal preview labels and action/DD-leg mismatch.
    - **Capture:** pause the actual scheduler between pick/decision/state saves and interleave the watchdog reads; assert pending/unverifiable rather than a fault or recovered notice. Pair with a qualified completed no-fault capture. A hand-built agreeing tuple or calls to individual writers in isolation do not exercise this gate.
    - **Dates/recovery:** invoke the actual date selector after a multi-day checker outage, with prior pending/unverifiable work, unreadable notification state, an incident older than three days, a replaced candidate and midnight rollover of restart/self incidents. Verify exact retired-target recovery/disposition, confirmed fake IDs and recurrence.
    - **Eligibility/receipt/mode:** all-rainout/resume-only and mixed eligible slates; late receipt without a decision; warnings before the entry window; both DD legs; wrong-date/invalid-type/schema records; a mode transition yesterday and within today; untagged active entry cron and non-executing command text. Preserve the no-games/postseason/research controls.
    - **Restart/dependency:** fresh-heartbeat churn at each registered threshold, missing/corrupt/reset baselines, disabled/misconfigured external churn, unavailable counter despite fresh sample-file mtime, no required polling after restart despite fresh heartbeat, old/future commit timestamps and failed restart after installing a previously authored commit. Include unavailable/malformed unit/head observations and healthy `uv` parent/Python child versus two independent trees.
    - **Cron and confinement:** drive the registered cron/CLI selection before 10:00, after 23:45, at off-grid cutoffs, across midnight/DST and during entry/grading/reconcile work. The actual install integration can belong to the whole-watchdog unit, but its gate evidence must be a named prerequisite rather than disappear. Run the new job's success, parse/config error, uncertain-send, cancellation, self/ping and exhausted-budget branches through the existing write gate, including its same-byte/write-undo/unlink and real-process red controls. Fake transport/auth/credential leaves must preclude ambient credential or network escape. The real seam gets separate direct tests.

    §8 describes gates 1 and 2 but no complete gate-3 cron/self demonstration. A mutation ledger with one mutant per table row is not sufficient if the table itself omits a registered trust boundary. Pin the added failure mechanisms and expected statuses/notification/ping outcomes, not merely a count of mutants.

## Required changes

This is **REVISE**, so these are acceptance conditions for the revised design, not verbatim SIGN WITH EDITS patches.

1. Specify an enforced end-to-end 120 s deadline, including admission/locks, predicate work, notification, persistence and ping. Preserve the 90 s notifier cap; propagate remaining time through actual DM retries/reads and ping, and define cancellation/uncertain-send and skipped-work outcomes.
2. Define W-self's expected due-work inventory, qualified completion rule and durable attempt/last-success/skip-streak schema. Make deadline exhaustion and missing due work suppress success independently of DM delivery. Keep completed business-fault observation distinct from checker execution failure, and retain the separate commissioning/rehearsal gate.
3. Close finalization explicitly: pending skip must expire at the next-day completion deadline; initial attempted sends must have a usable, non-resetting age rule or explicit unavailable evidence; private lock ambiguity and nonterminal preview labels need exact dispositions.
4. Register qualifying evidence/closure for every cross-file verdict in this unit. If a producer prerequisite is required, name its separately reviewed contract; otherwise limit the verdict to pending/unverifiable and alert overdue completion. Do not assign this obligation to deferred W-state.
5. Replace notification-only/date-only discovery and silent three-day retirement with a complete unresolved-date/selection and incident-disposition contract, including notification-state failure, missed runs, changed selections and midnight recovery.
6. Define eligible opportunities, independent receipt timing, the entry warning window and schedule freshness for active prior-date incidents. Explicitly disposition unavailable scheduled-check observation rather than claiming complete W-deliver fidelity from sleeping wake alone.
7. Replace the commit-time deployment assertion and heartbeat-as-poll assertion with qualified evidence, or explicit deferred/unverifiable dispositions. Define external monitor configuration/sample qualification and per-input unavailable results.
8. Define historical intent attribution and actual installed entry-cron parsing, including untagged active jobs, without assuming an intent-change timestamp exists.
9. Add field/date/identity/status/action/leg validation after compatibility parsers, and define unavailable-input precedence over no-games/skip/all-clear results.
10. Close the DM credential subprocess escape and declare all network-leaf contracts and the real-process tests/boundary.
11. Extend the gate and mutation plan with the concrete counterexamples in finding 12 and the relevant registered cron integration prerequisite. Preserve R1/R5 writer confinement and R6's prohibition on additional authenticated contest work.
