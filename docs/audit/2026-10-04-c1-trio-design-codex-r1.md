## Findings (A)

**A1 — High: the prior has no defined likelihood scale.** Registration lines 14–15 specify Normal(0, 0.5²) and an “equal-date-weighted log-likelihood,” without defining whether date means are summed or averaged, or row weights are normalized to the number of rows. These produce different candidates, not an implementation detail. Synthetic identical dates with p=0.7 and realized fraction 0.6 gave MAP a = −0.023747 when date means are averaged, −0.282213 when 30 date means are summed, and −0.440609 when 6,000 row likelihoods are summed. No real data were used. Fix the objective and interval construction before fitting (A-E1).

The choice of one intercept and the specified prior can remain a deliberately restricted candidate. W1.3's slope interval covering 1 does **not** establish that slope distortion is absent or that a constant probability offset exists: its memo explicitly says this at `docs/audit/2026-10-04-w13-distinguishing-tests.md:61–70`. A constant logit shift is neither a fixed percentage-point shift nor necessarily downward. Do not reopen a slope search; correct the justification.

**A2 — High: “last write that day” is not a prospective/policy-read witness.** Lines 20/28 and 73 inherit a mutable daily slate, without checking that a row's game was still eligible when that slate was written. `src/bts/slate.py:10–13,61–68` overwrites the file; `src/bts/orchestrator.py:276–300` saves it before eligibility/strategy selection. It therefore does not establish the claimed “population the policy reads,” final action or decision-time availability. The frozen bridge explicitly retains this limitation (`docs/audit/2026-10-04-benchmark-bridge.md:75`). A late rerun could supply a started-game forecast to the calibration sample. Fix a pregame eligibility rule and label this forecast population accurately; rank before joining outcomes, with no substitution for a no_pa/unknown winner (A-E2).

**A3 — Medium: support, grading and the 30/60 split are not fully fixed.** Lines 30–33/57–60 count from the first slate and skip missing dates, leaving unclear whether missing dates advance the window. The “fit after date 30” job conflicts with waiting until date 40 for 25 graded fit dates. There is no test-date support floor. A test supported by only a few dates could pass a percentile interval. Finite but out-of-range p also needs rejection; 0/1 need a logit convention.

There is an additional inherited grading trap: `scripts/audit/benchmark_bridge/core.py:62–66` returns no_hit for n_pa>0, n_hits=0 even when source_complete=False. Reuse of that function alone does not prove a game was finished; a partial scoreless game can later contain a hit. Require independently complete, settled sources before assigning hit/no_hit for this study. “Analysis after 08:00” alone does not supply that check. Fix these definitions and freeze consumed bytes once (A-E2/A-E3).

**A4 — Medium: the practical threshold has a rationale, but the guardrail and precision claim do not.** The −0.001 threshold is a permissible owner-facing practical bar, not a measured achievable effect. Its Taylor example is approximately correct (0.03²/(2·0.7·0.3) = 0.002143), but the 2026 +0.031 residual has different weights and does not imply a uniform offset in this population. The point-only rank-1 guardrail at line 45 can pass despite substantial uncertainty about rank-1 harm. With one winner per date, thousands of slate rows do not create thousands of independent rank-1 observations. Add a one-sided upper-bound guardrail and an honest date-level precision rationale; insufficient precision means inconclusive (A-E4).

The date-bootstrap must preserve repeated draw multiplicities. The reusable bridge helper concatenates repeated original dates (`core.py:123–135`); passing a statistic that regroups by the original date and then averages date means discards those multiplicities. A synthetic draw [0,0,1] then incorrectly becomes 0.5 rather than 1/3. Bootstrap the precomputed date differences directly. This is a demonstrated reuse hazard, not a claim that unbuilt C1 code already has the defect.

## Findings (B)

**B1 — High/blocking: PA rows cannot establish the proposed starting lineup or first-inning order certificate.** Lines 42–46 assert more than the source retains:

- `build.parse_game_feed` iterates allPlays and `build_season` preserves that row order (`src/bts/data/build.py:178–181,380–396`). That is a verified producer property of this source version, not proof of the actual historical files' producer/order.
- `_get_lineup_positions` reduces 900 and 901 to slot 9 (`build.py:100–114`). A starter removed before his first PA is absent from the parquet; his replacement becomes the first batter seen. An in-memory parser probe produced **identical PA records** from (i) batter 209 starting in slot 9 and (ii) starter 109 with zero PA, replaced by 209 before that slot's first PA. This is lost information, not an ordering error.
- `src/bts/data/schema.py:20–71` retains no inning, at-bat/play index, starting-batter flag, original battingOrder suffix or starting-pitcher flag. The first nine slot appearances can occur across several innings. The proposed “in the first inning” check is unavailable, and requiring all nine there would reject ordinary games. Conversely, “each side's first-PA pitcher must be one pitcher” is tautological if it means the first row, and cannot certify chronology.
- The first retained PA's pitcher is an approximation to the starter, not independently witnessed starter identity, especially because non-PA events are removed. Reusing the existing diagnostic's `.first()` (`src/bts/simulate/backtest_blend.py:626–652`) does not cure this.

Smallest sound fix: require authoritative lineup/starter and order metadata tied to the input games, or defer. Do not silently retain up to 1% bad games. No claim is made about how often these cases occur in the unread parquets (B-E1).

**B2 — Medium: the formula is sound under its assumptions, but the candidate is incomplete and its rationale is false in general.** For a specified conditional P(N), nonnegative integer N_s≤N and conditional independent PA Bernoulli rates, line 31 computes the intended P(at least one hit | N≥1). A hand fixture with P(N=2)=P(N=4)=0.5, q_s=0.2, q_r=0.3, slot=1 and BF=10 gives p=0.5232. This is a formula check, not evidence that constant PA hazards or the deterministic starter split are accurate.

The conditional target is coherent with the registered no_pa exclusion. Conditioning historical labels on N≥1 is permitted for fitting that conditional count distribution; line 38's blanket prohibition on *any* such filter contradicts line 27 and should distinguish historical target construction from prospective selection. Unknown slots, missing/invalid BF, N>8 and same-date starts are not specified. BF is not shown to be “already” a live computed field merely because PA history and pitcher identity exist. A row `shift(1)` can admit a same-date start; require strictly earlier dates. Production shares the **single-model** reliever q across blend models (`predict.py:766–779`); per-model starter q is not retained in the daily slate (`slate.py:35–40`). Freeze these conventions and archive the actual inputs (B-E2).

Lines 77–78 overstate Jensen and the ranking mechanism. With q_s=0.1, q_r=0.5, starter cutoff 2, and N equally 1/3, the count-distribution p is 0.3475, **above** the same-mean plug-in p=0.19. The two-rate, piecewise allocation does not have the general one-rate Jensen guarantee. Another synthetic example used identical deterministic N=4 for two slot-1 batters: baseline A=0.688231 versus B=0.667029; candidate A=0.640000 versus B=0.648470 when their starter allocations are 2 and 4. Ranking reverses with **zero count variance in both**. Mean, allocation and q_s/q_r interactions also matter.

**B3 — High: deadline selection is not necessarily pre-lock, and per-candidate stitching is not a live slate.** Lines 55–58 select the last row before game first pitch−5, but an actual production commit can occur earlier. A 14:00 commit and 16:50 refresh for a 17:00 game pass that rule even though the second information set is post-commit. Merely excluding “after first pitch” also leaves the last five-minute interval undefined in the prose. Use strict `< cutoff`, input-availability and game-status evidence, and a recorded terminal decision boundary.

Selecting a different last run for each candidate can assemble probabilities/lineups that never coexisted; the proposed rank-1 guardrail and disagreement rates would then describe an invented slate. Keep a common complete run for slate-level ranking. Missing shadow rows must not select an older, favorable forecast or remove an otherwise scoreable baseline row. The archive needs stable `(run_id,date,batter_id,game_pk)` identity, whole-run completion, ordered pool, recipe/model/artifact provenance and the actual q inputs, not just two scalar probabilities (B-E3).

**B4 — High: matching pick/slate bytes does not prove unchanged decision behavior.** Line 56's test can miss a changed skip/defer/double/delivery branch, a changed transport call or a cached-fallback path that writes no new pick. It can also fail on an otherwise unchanged path because `save_slate` obtains a real wall timestamp. Use fixed clocks and compare selection/action/state/eligibility, production decision files and delivery traces as well as file bytes. Include failed/slow shadow, cutoff crossing, skip, private, cached fallback and restart controls. Do not insert a blocking shadow computation or exception into the active decision path and call its overhead “negligible” without evidence (B-E3).

**B5 — High: a behavior-preserving boundary remap is not available for this candidate; the June-null condition is not implemented.** Section 7 borrows 4a's scalar monotone-remapping option. B changes candidates differently and can reverse ranks, so no global boundary remap preserves its selection, partners or action. Any production use is a prediction/decision change needing applicable downstream replay and Eric's D7 approval.

The all-row proper-score gate can pass entirely by reducing probability levels, while rank-1 discrimination still shows nothing beyond the June PA-tilt null. Reporting that null as context (line 74) does not test a residual beyond it. The smallest non-expanding fix is to distinguish a positive **forecast-only** result from satisfaction of the plan's residual-lever/production gate; do not manufacture a new discrimination candidate or silently waive the kill condition. Add fixed support, paired missingness, precision and the rank-1 regression bound as for A (B-E4/B-E5).

## Findings (C)

**C1 — High: unchanged receipts have no retained object binding, and marker equality is time-local.** Lines 36–39 specify `stored_path` or the string “unchanged.” A previous-marker hash does not identify an extant retained file. The current code returns unchanged solely on marker equality, even if that snapshot is missing/corrupt (`src/bts/leaderboard/static_capture.py:125–129`). A prior receipt may also predate instrumentation. The unchanged completion must name and verify retained bytes. Earlier receipts cannot all equal the *current* marker after subsequent content changes; record the resulting marker value at each completion instead.

Hashing the original compressed response before decompression and hashing the decoded payload before validation is sensible. `_default_fetch` currently returns only already-decoded bytes (`static_capture.py:88–91`), so the fetch seam must change to expose HTTP metadata and wire-body bytes. “Stored .gz bytes ... cannot bind” is incorrect: those exact bytes can be hashed; gzip timestamps affect reproducibility, not hash binding. A decoded digest identifies payload content, while an optional compressed digest identifies the stored representation. Count wire/decoded/stored lengths separately, and do not claim a complete-response hash for a partial/failed read (C-E1).

**C2 — High: the stop is an intended behavior, not a closed stdlib/cron contract yet.** A synthetic `urllib.error.HTTPError(429)` passed to the current capture_one became ordinary fetch_error; the generic catch swallows its classification (`static_capture.py:114–117`), capture_all continues (`153–154`), and main may return success (`203–204`). The new tests must exercise that native exception seam, not only an invented injected result status.

The persistent marker and exit 3 are appropriate. They need one writer lock across the preflight check, fetches, receipts and marker updates, plus a recheck before each request. The cron line currently has no static-capture flock (`scripts/cron-setup-hetzner.sh:66`). Otherwise overlapping/manual runs can continue after another process trips the stop. Record the limit before reading/decompressing its error body; classify 403/429 ahead of generic errors, abort immediately, and make durable receipt/stop failures fail closed. The completion must repeat an unambiguous attempt id; intent must be durable before network starts. Skipped later feeds have no fetch attempt, so “one per feed per run” must not imply fabricated completions. Add the watchdog condition concretely: the current rank-2 table contains no static-capture stop check. Its alert also must not substitute for C1's cycle-wide pause (C-E2/X-E1).

**C3 — High/blocking: exact matching on a selected subset is not all-player round/game identification.** Lines 68–78 compare only players present in both sheets, but the broader sheet's proposed benefit is the **unlisted** players. A provider that always copies today's values for popular players and uses another round for everyone else passes 100%/100%. A stale reference sheet can also produce perfect agreement. Missing tomorrow rows can manufacture “exactly one” if absence is treated as inequality. An unrestricted “fixed function of capture time” fitted to the same finite captures can be a lookup table and pass by construction. No particular rollover rule, timezone, derivation/validation separation, missing-date denominator or coverage floor is fixed.

Smallest safe fix: retain this as an outcome-free concordance diagnostic, not a Bindable gate; label broader-sheet binding **not established** unless independent round identity evidence exists. Preserve most-selected-only coverage and each row's inferred-versus-witnessed distinction. Do not make a searched rollover rule into the preregistered identity test (C-E3).

Outcome-free status does not extend X-23. Publish X-33 with probability-field/identity reads and no labels, fitting of a blend, score or outcome-bearing joins. ### A-E1 — Replace §1 and the first two “In plain words” bullets

> **The motivation.** W1.3's restricted 2026 primary support had a candidate-row-weighted stated-minus-realized point residual of +0.031, and a native rank-1 point residual of +0.060 on a different population/weighting. These descriptive figures motivate testing the registered calibration slot; they do not establish a uniform or stable bias on the new equal-date population.
>
> **The test.** We fit one logit intercept on fixed 2027 contest dates 1–30 and test it on fixed dates 31–90. The fitted map may raise or lower probabilities; it is not a constant percentage-point correction.
>
> ## 1. The map
> Candidate: g_a(p) = logistic(logit(p) + a), with one intercept and a Normal(0, 0.5²) prior. Identity is the comparator. For fitting, p is clipped to [10⁻¹⁵, 1−10⁻¹⁵]; the same clipping convention is used for both arms' log loss. Invalid, nonnumeric, boolean, non-finite or out-of-[0,1] probabilities are excluded and counted before clipping. For displayed mapped probabilities, g_a(0)=0 and g_a(1)=1 by continuity.
> The MAP maximizes Σ_d [(1/n_d) Σ_i {y_di log g_a(p_di) + (1−y_di) log(1−g_a(p_di))}] − a²/(2·0.5²), summing over eligible fit dates. Date means are summed, not averaged; the weights are not rescaled to the candidate-row count. This is a fixed weighted likelihood with a regularizing prior, not an independently calibrated posterior uncertainty claim. Report the MAP; any normalized weighted-likelihood posterior interval is descriptive and is labelled as such.
> No slope challenger is included. W1.3's D slope interval covers 1 but does not establish absence of slope distortion. Intercept-only is the deliberately restricted candidate specified by plan row 4a. The 2026 residuals motivate this test; they establish neither a uniform offset nor a validated correction. Ranking and tie order are taken from the original eligible forecast pool before outcome exclusions.

### A-E4 — Replace §4's primary/guardrail/rationale bullets; retain its disposition table and descriptive secondary list

> Primary: mean of the test dates' within-date log-loss differences, map minus identity. Resample this date-difference vector with replacement 10,000 times at seed 20270101; each sampled occurrence retains its multiplicity. Use the 2.5th/97.5th percentiles as the 95% interval. Condition on the once-fitted map; this interval excludes fit uncertainty and cross-date dependence.
> Guardrail: the 95th percentile of the corresponding 10,000-draw bootstrap of original rank-1 date differences must be ≤0.005 nats. Rank-1 draws use the fixed known-winner support and seed 20270103. A point estimate alone cannot pass the guardrail. Positive requires the registered primary practical threshold and upper bound plus this guardrail; difference ≥0 is negative; every other case is inconclusive.
> The −0.001-nat practical threshold and 0.005-nat regression limit are fixed judgments, not measured effects. A hypothetical uniform 0.03 correction near p=0.7 yields approximately 0.00214 nats; the descriptive 2026 residual does not establish that assumption on this equal-date population.
> Precision rationale (assumptions only): for 60 independent paired dates, a normal sign-detection calculation at two-sided 5% and 80% power gives MDE ≈2.802·σ_date/√60. Assumed date-difference SDs of 0.005, 0.010 and 0.020 imply MDEs about 0.00181, 0.00362 and 0.00723 nats. These are sensitivities, not measured variance or power. They do not certify power for the full practical-threshold/guardrail gate; an effect exactly at the point threshold has approximately a one-half probability of clearing that point condition under symmetric sampling. The study can be inconclusive despite a useful effect. Do not change thresholds or enlarge the window after seeing outcomes.
