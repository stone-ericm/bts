## Findings

1. **Blocking — the primary arm does not measure the stated pre-pick identity, and the two arms do not bound production.** Section 1 says the catcher is the one production would actually know before the pick; §3 instead supplies a final-boxscore identity. In the inspected code, `predict._fetch_game_slots` constructs opposing team/pitcher fields but no opposing-catcher field (lines 532–553). `scheduler._lock_decision_from_predictions` gates on the primary and double-down batters' projected-lineup status (lines 1339–1352, 1378–1388), and `lineup_collect.CollectionState.write_jsonl` saves times and flags, without player identities (lines 109–116). These establish the availability gap in the supplied architecture; I did not inspect production archives or quantify scratches. A scratch can change the final starter after the relevant posting/pick snapshot. The claim that the two arms “bound that mix” is also false: in a synthetic two-candidate ranking, posted scores X=.9/Y=.8 and projected scores X=.7/Y=.6 both pick the hitting X; mixed scores X=.7/Y=.8 pick the missing Y. Both endpoint hit rates are 1 and the hybrid's is 0. The diff explicitly makes this a proxy experiment and leaves actual pre-pick availability and mixed serving behavior for later gating.

2. **Blocking — five dates is not the implementation's five observations; an absent-catcher lookup must not use the last shifted row.** `screen.framing_by` (lines 109–118) takes PA-rate means per build catcher/date and then `shift(1).expanding(min_periods=5).mean()`. Pandas counts nonmissing daily rates. My synthetic execution of that exact source function returned NaN after five prior dates containing only four finite rates. Two same-date game values .6 and .8 became one daily rate .7. A strict prefix lookup matched all nine observed synthetic rows, including their NaNs. On a date when the named catcher was absent, its correctly accumulated prior-date value was .4666667; taking the last already-shifted feature would return .38. Changing a future daily rate left the earlier lookup unchanged. Equality is with a catcher id present in the build's date group, not automatically every actual catcher who caught that day. The diff defines the daily aggregation, nonmissing minimum, strict cutoff, doubleheader treatment and lookup construction. It also forbids reassigning historical catcher ids from the arm's table.

3. **Blocking — the starter rule needs an exact feed field, cardinality and failure policy.** Ending `00` alone does not define an eligible batting slot, and “first listed game position” leaves `position` versus `allPositions` unspecified. The repo's existing `_get_starting_catchers` (build.py lines 117–135) searches any C entry and takes the first player in dictionary order, then falls back to primary position; it does not identify a batting-order starter. Executing that source on the same synthetic players returned substitute 99 when it was inserted first and starter 11 after reversing insertion order. The new rule is a sensible conservative starter proxy when canonical starting batting orders and correctly ordered game positions are available. It can fail with missing/malformed position lists, ambiguous candidates, a final lineup revision, a starter who changes defensive positions, or a catcher who was not an original batting starter. No real feed was supplied or read, so I cannot certify MLB's array order as chronological first defensive alignment. The diff names `allPositions[0].code`, canonical slot strings, exact ids, null reasons and the uniqueness rule, and labels the ordering assumption. It refuses an entirely unavailable primary catcher contrast instead of calling predictions through all-missing routes a successful catcher test.

4. **Blocking — projection ambiguities and overstated measurement descriptions.** “Skip” can mean either remove unknown games before choosing ten or choose ten games and then ignore unknown ids. A synthetic history with ten known starts followed by ten unknown starts yields catcher 1 under the first interpretation and no projection under the second. The original also leaves the small-window rule and same-date recency ordering unspecified. The diff chooses the last ten games first, earlier official dates only, deterministic game ordering, latest-start tie breaking, and no window extension. This is a fixed research choice, not something demonstrated superior by the supplied data. It explicitly allows a prior-year id without adding an undeclared roster lookup.
