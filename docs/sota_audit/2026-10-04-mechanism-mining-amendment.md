It is chosen retrospectively, so the survivorship and right-truncation caveat is stated in every report (protocol "Methodology Constraints").

## A2. Consensus slots, ties and legal pairs
- **Slot 1 (primary)** = the modal `batter_id` among cohort users' pick_number 1 for the date.
- **Slot 2 (double-down)** = the modal `batter_id` among cohort users' pick_number 2. When it equals the slot-1 consensus batter, the next modal batter is used (a legal pair needs two distinct batters). Ties are resolved as below.
- **Ties:** a modal tie is broken by the lowest `batter_id`, the script's existing deterministic rule. Every unit whose consensus came from a tie break is flagged `consensus_tie_broken`.
  - **Primary:** all units.
  - **Sensitivity:** tie-broken units excluded, with BH/BY recomputed over that family and reported beside the primary.
  - A cell that nominates only through tie-broken units is reported as such.
- **Production slots** follow the protocol: primary → 1, double_down → 2. A date where production made no double-down has no slot-2 unit.

## A3. Ranked model surfaces (variables 13–14)
- The protocol admits only at-lock or manifest-proven ranked surfaces.
- **The surface:** the served full slates `data/picks/slates/<date>.json` (`bts_slate_v1`, from 2026-06-11), on dates where the slate is **selection-consistent** in the sense of the frozen W1.2 bridge design (§2: decision-first, serialized-probability match).
- **What they are:** the production serving output written before selection; that is their manifest. They are not proven to be the exact lock-time attempt; the W1.2 limits apply.
- **Elsewhere:** dates before 6/11, or with no selection-consistent slate, use `missing_surface`, as the protocol specifies.
